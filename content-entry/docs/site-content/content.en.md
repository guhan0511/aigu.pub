## 5. Detail Pages

### 5.1 Education Page (Alumni, education.html)

Page title: 2019—2026: From BISU to the world.
Subtitle: Seven years, half in Beijing, half quietly rewritten by an exchange. If you've also taken classes in these two cities, we've probably crossed paths.
Tags: 2019—2026 / Beijing · Paris / BISU · ESSEC

**Timeline — seven years, three chapters:**
- 2019—2023 | Bachelor's · School of Economics, BISU: Admitted to BISU as a Tianjin examinee, circling back to Beijing, the city of my birth. Bachelor's degree in Economics — also "the hardest class" to go through, having lived through the entire COVID pandemic, and serving at the Beijing 2022 Winter Olympics venues in the capital with the school's support. From a freshman for whom "economics" was still a vague word, to a senior who could start using frameworks to understand how the world works — four years of slowly narrowing interest into direction.
- 2023—2026 | Master's · School of Economics, BISU: After the graduate entrance exam to two top finance/trade universities didn't work out, stayed on at BISU's School of Economics for a master's degree, graduating in 2026 with a thesis on the impact of AI on global value chain networks. Outside the classroom during the master's years, more time went into real-world projects — startups, internships, overseas expansion — with frameworks learned in class repeatedly tested out in the field.
- Fall 2024 | Exchange · ESSEC Business School, France (Grande École Exchange): With the school's support and strong personal motivation, first selected as a Paris Olympics intern, then stayed on for a semester's exchange at a French business school, moving my base of life from Beijing to the Paris suburbs. Classes were only scheduled Tuesday through Thursday, so every week turned into a four-day mini-holiday; it was also during this time that I met a BISU alum from the class of '99 majoring in French — an experience I later turned into a longer travel-study essay.

**Beyond the classroom: one semester, half of Europe:**
The pace of the exchange semester unexpectedly gave me a lot of "mini-holidays." So starting from Sicily, Italy (visited an old BISU classmate there, and for a moment felt like I'd stepped into a scene from The Godfather), I went on to Rome and Florence; within France, traveled along the Côte d'Azur through Nice, Cannes, Monaco, Antibes and Menton; also went to Normandy, and took a solo trip to Ghent and Bruges in Belgium.
The Switzerland leg was hosted by friends — hiking on Mount Rigi, and a proper Fondue dinner; Berlin, Germany was explored entirely on foot, thirty thousand steps in a day; the UK started with swing dancing in Manchester, then London, then Edinburgh; Christmas was spent at a friend's place in Denmark — probably the purest Christmas I've ever had.

**What I took away:**
Beijing gave me frameworks and methods; Paris gave me a way of living where "leave your schedule a little looser, and the world will find its way in." Layering these two rhythms together is roughly how I handle work and life now — plan carefully, but leave room to arrive somewhere unexpected.

### 5.2 Work Page (Fellow Travelers, work.html)

Page title: From growth to going overseas: doing real work in real markets.
Subtitle: Didn't want to turn this page into a resume. Would rather make clear: where I am now, what paths I've walked before, and why I believe these things are worth doing.
Tags: Growth / Automotive Aftermarket / Southeast Asia / Startup

**Now / Before:**
- **Now**: After graduating with a master's in 2026, joined X-motors (Bosch's national distributor in Indonesia) for automotive aftermarket expansion overseas — leveraging a strong domestic auto parts supply chain to start a franchise business for auto repair and modification in Indonesia, capturing incremental market share. Currently on a cross-functional growth team, responsible for ad buying, marketing strategy and user growth, dealing with Meta, Google, TikTok, customer service systems and Google Forms every day.
- **Before**: In spring 2025, commuted an hour and a half each day from east fifth ring road Beijing to Wudaokou for an internship at MiraclePlus, following the entire evolution of AI development and sitting face-to-face with startup mentors and outstanding founders — that was when I stopped wanting to compete in the existing job market, and instead wanted to personally verify the full loop from discovering a need to delivering a product. During the internship, took part in two startup camps, watched 50+ projects land, and brought startup talks to 11 cities and 28 universities nationwide, looking for the young people on campus who might build the next unicorn. Around the same time, a BISU alum from the class of '99 I'd met during the France exchange asked me to help restart the Beijing office — so I jumped in, spending three months building a team, renting an office, and handling tax and legal compliance, bringing the brand back to life to run a high-end boutique government/enterprise small-group travel business (Europa Holiday Travel Agency).

**Field Notes — those days spent looking for people on campus:**
In the first half of 2025, visited 11 cities and 28 universities researching the state of innovation education. A question I kept coming back to during that time: how can we find more people like Liang Wenfeng or Wang Xingxing earlier, while they're still in high school or college.
At the fall 2025 startup camp, I watched a "startup gala" from the VIP seats reserved for university partners — three-plus hours, 54 project pitches, ranging from electronic-pet social apps, travel pilots, cancer patient communities, to a disease-preventing smart ring, a desktop companion robot, ultra-low-orbit satellites, fishing, AI agriculture, and biomimetic flapping-wing devices... founders' imagination for the world is always wilder than expected.

### 5.3 Moments Page (Shared Memories / Olympics, moments.html)

Page title: I was there at two Olympic Games.
Subtitle: In 2008 I was just a spectator outside the Bird's Nest; in 2022 and 2024, I became someone working on-site. If these events have moved you too, we probably share a memory.
Tags: Beijing 2022 / Paris 2024 / OBS

**Four archive entries:**
- **2008 / BEIJING — The first time, just a spectator**: At seven years old, my parents took me to check out the Bird's Nest and the Water Cube. I had no idea that, over a decade later, I'd stand at an Olympics again in a completely different role.
- **2022 / BEIJING — Winter Olympics and Paralympics volunteer**: Under full closed-loop management during the pandemic, handling dedicated pickups and drop-offs for athletes and officials; I served 1,000+ foreign guests at a contracted hotel, while also serving as the hotel's "COVID liaison," responsible for epidemic monitoring and patient transfers — a stretch of time when my mask never came off, yet I was close to the world. On closing ceremony day, I watched this grand farewell play out live at the Bird's Nest. I still keep the media sponsor badge I received.
- **2024 / PARIS — OBS Archive Team Intern**: As one of 30 interns, employed by Olympic Broadcasting Services, handled global broadcast footage tagging for golf and surfing at the Le Bourget Exhibition Centre (IBC) in Paris. 16 days, 12 time zones, 120+ athletes, 100+ hours of footage clips, 0 errors — behind these numbers was someone still double-checking timecodes at 3am.
- **2024 / TEAHUPO'O — Sunrise on Tahiti**: The surfing competition venue was all the way on Tahiti, a full 12 time zones from Paris. To hit footage delivery deadlines, I saw the sunrise there many times — probably the most luxurious memory this job left me with.

**What's at the core is being there in person:**
The biggest thing I took away from two Olympics wasn't a line on a resume, but a new understanding of the word "on-site" — a major event is never a one-person stage, but the result of countless collaborators meshing together along a precise timeline. A big part of sport's appeal lies right there: it connects the world in the most direct way possible.
If this kind of behind-the-scenes work interests you, OBS recruits freelancers on an ongoing basis — check out careers.obs.tv for more.

### 5.4 Interests Page (interests.html)

Page title: Outside of work, I take life seriously too.
Subtitle: Sweat-inducing sports, channel-switching music, slowly accumulated self-growth — if you're into any one of these, we'll probably click.
Tags: Outdoor / Music / Self-growth

**Outdoor — Sweat first, clear head after** (Canyoning / Hiking / Ultimate Frisbee / Skiing):
I like sports that pull you back into your body — canyoning demands full focus on the water flow and rock face under your feet, hiking makes your breathing turn honest as the altitude climbs, and there's no spare brainpower on an ultimate frisbee field to think about work. Going skiing in winter is the most consistent ritual of recent years: in the snow haze you can't see anything clearly, yet somehow everything becomes clear in your head.
Quote: Exercise isn't for staying in shape, it's for staying clear-headed.

**Music — Switching selves through different channels** (Jazz / City Pop / Rock / Afro):
Jazz suits working late alone; City Pop is that relaxed weekend morning feeling, good sunlight, nothing to do; rock is reserved for moments when emotions need an outlet; Afro's groove always makes you want to move. I don't keep a fixed playlist — it's more like switching channels based on mood. Music is probably the fastest way to flip someone from one state into another.

**Self-growth — Treating growth as something you can keep practicing** (Reading / Investing / Fitness):
There's a loose thread running through the books I read in 2025: how not to waste your own judgment under new tools and new rules — from the obsession with aesthetics in Hackers & Painters, to the repeated emphasis on "finding real demand" in Real Demand, to the awareness of cognitive limits in Awakening to Wealth, they're all pointing in the same direction. Investing and fitness are, in a way, another form of reading — both require long-termism, anti-fragility, and compounding to see results.
Quote: It's hard to earn money beyond your own cognition, so build that cognition solidly first.
